Articles: a / an / the / no article 冠词

An article is a small word placed before a noun to show whether that noun is specific or general. Every singular noun phrase makes one of four choices.

ChoiceWhen to use itExample
a / anNon-specific singular countable noun — first mention, or one of manyI saw a dog. She ate an apple.
theSpecific to both writer and reader — already mentioned, unique, or clear from contextI saw a dog. The dog was wearing a red collar.
∅ (no article)General plural or uncountable nouns, and most proper nounsDogs make loyal pets. Honesty matters.
Sound, not spelling: "an" is chosen by the sound that follows — an hour (silent h), but a university ("yoo-" sound).
"an" 由后面的发音决定，不是拼写 — an hour（h不发音），但 a university（发"yoo"音）。

Pronoun–Antecedent Agreement 代词与先行词一致

The antecedent is the noun a pronoun refers back to. A pronoun must match its antecedent in number (singular/plural), person, and gender.

Five rules that cover almost every question

RuleExample
Singular antecedent → singular pronoun; plural → pluralThe girl lost her keys. The girls lost their keys.
Indefinite pronouns (each, every, everyone, someone) are singular — singular they is now widely acceptedEach of the players brought their own water.
With or / nor, the pronoun agrees with the nearer antecedentNeither the coach nor the players brought their bags.
Collective nouns (team, class, committee) acting as one unit take a singular pronounThe team celebrated its victory.
Avoid vague reference — the reader must know exactly who the pronoun meansUnclear: "Maya told Sara that she had won." — who won?
Quick method: underline the antecedent first, then ask "singular or plural? specific or general?" before choosing the word.
先划出先行词，再问"单数还是复数？特指还是泛指？"，最后选词。

Twelve Tenses = Three Times × Four Aspects 十二种时态 = 三种时间 × 四种体

Every English verb form makes one choice of time (when) and one choice of aspect (how the action unfolds).

SimpleContinuousPerfectPerfect Continuous
Pastwrotewas/were writinghad writtenhad been writing
Presentwrite/writesam/is/are writinghave/has writtenhave/has been writing
Futurewill writewill be writingwill have writtenwill have been writing

What Each Aspect Means 每种"体"的含义

AspectMeaningSignal words
SimpleFacts, habits, routines, completed eventsevery day, usually, yesterday
ContinuousAction in progress at a particular momentright now, while, at that moment
PerfectConnects two points in time — completed before/relevant to another momentalready, just, ever/never, by the time
Perfect ContinuousEmphasizes how long an action has continued up to a pointsince, for, all morning
Find the signal words first — they usually name the tense for you.
先找信号词——它们通常能告诉你该用哪种时态。

Four Sentence Errors the Quiz Will Test 测验会考的四种句子错误

Run-on: The test was hard I studied all night.
Fixed: The test was hard. I studied all night.
Comma splice: The test was hard, I studied all night.
Fixed: The test was hard, so I studied all night.
Fragment: Because I studied all night.
Fixed: Because I studied all night, I felt ready.
Tense shift: She walked into the room and sits down.
Fixed: She walked into the room and sat down.

The Plot Diagram: Six Stages 情节图：六个阶段

StageJob
ExpositionIntroduces characters, setting, and situation — the reader's "before" picture
Inciting IncidentThe single event that disturbs normal life and launches the conflict
Rising ActionComplications and choices that raise the stakes, event by event
ClimaxThe turning point — the moment the outcome of the conflict is decided
Falling ActionConsequences unfold, tension eases, loose ends are gathered
ResolutionThe conflict is settled — the story closes, showing the "new normal"
The climax is not the most exciting scene by default — it is the scene where the main conflict is decided. Ask: "After this moment, could the story still go either way?" If no, that was the climax.
高潮不一定是最刺激的场景——而是决定主要冲突结果的场景。问自己："这一刻之后，故事还能有别的走向吗？"如果不能，那就是高潮。

Six Elements Every Story Is Built From 每个故事的六大要素

ElementWhat it is
CharacterThe people in the story — led by the protagonist, opposed by the antagonist
SettingWhere and when the story happens — place, time, mood
PlotThe sequence of events, arranged for effect (the six-stage diagram above)
ConflictThe problem or struggle that drives the story — internal or external
ThemeThe story's central message, written as a full sentence — not one word
Point of ViewWho tells the story and how much they know

Conflict Types

Person vs. Person · Person vs. Self · Person vs. Nature · Person vs. Society · Person vs. Technology

Points of View

First person — narrator is a character ("I caught the bus"). Third limited — outside narrator, inside one mind. Third omniscient — all-knowing narrator who can enter any character's thoughts.
