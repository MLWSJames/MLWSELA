The Eight Word Classes · 八大词类

Every English word belongs to one of eight parts of speech. Click 🔊 to hear each word pronounced.

01 · Parts of Speech · 词性

We sort words into groups so we know the job each word does in a sentence. The same word can do different jobs — check the job, not just the word.

ClassJobExamples
Noun (N)names a person, place, thing or ideateacher, school, laptop, happiness
Pronoun (P)stands in place of a nounhe, she, it, they
Verb (V)shows an action or a state of beingrun, think, is, was writing
Adjective (Adj)describes or changes a noun/pronounblue, gigantic, confident
Adverb (Adv)describes a verb, adjective or word groupsoftly, quickly, yesterday
Preposition (Pr)shows position, direction or relationshipto, at, with, against
Conjunction (C)joins words, phrases and clausesand, but, because, although
Interjection (I)expresses sudden or strong emotionWow! Ouch! Oh dear!
💡 Test: Can you put "the" or "a" in front of the word? Then it is probably a noun. · 如果一个词前面可以加 "the" 或 "a"，它很可能是名词。

Four Noun Types · 四种名词

Abstract (an idea you cannot touch): pride, courage, danger  |  Collective (a group): a herd of sheep, a flock of birds  |  Compound (two words joined): airplane, keyboard  |  Proper (a specific name, capitalized): China, Harry Potter

Pronouns & Articles · 代词与冠词

Pronouns replace a noun so we don't repeat the same name: "Maria lost her keys, but she found them later." The = one particular thing ("the dog is eating my homework"). A/an = any one of a general group ("a dog is eating my homework"); use an before a vowel sound ("an apple a day").

Adjectives & Verbs · 形容词与动词

Adjectives answer "what kind?", "which one?" or "how many?" and usually sit before a noun or after "be". Verbs show a doing or a being, and change form for person, tense, and aspect (I walk / she walks / I walked / I am walking / I have walked). Modal verbs (can, could, may, might, must, shall, should, will, would) help the main verb and never stand alone: "You should study before the quiz."

Adverbs & Prepositions · 副词与介词

Most adverbs end in -ly (softly, quickly), but some do not (well, fast, never, now). Some adjectives also end in -ly (likely, friendly) — always ask what the word modifies. Prepositions link a noun to the rest of the sentence and show position or direction: "She stubbed her toe against the door."

📌 Contractions drop letters and mark the gap with an apostrophe: don't = do + not. A contraction counts as its own word — never split it in two when labeling. · 缩写词用撇号标记省略的字母，标注时不要拆分成两个词。

Root Words, Prefixes & Suffixes · 词根、前缀与后缀

A root word is the simplest form with no prefix or suffix (happy, dress, manage). A prefix is added before the root to change its meaning (un + happy = unhappy). A suffix is added after the root, and suffixes often change the word class (happy → happiness): this is the key to word forms below.

Phrases & Clauses · 短语与从句

A phrase is a group of words with no subject-verb pair ("in the first place"). A clause has both a subject and a verb. An independent clause can stand alone as a full sentence ("The woman sang."). A dependent clause cannot stand alone — it needs an independent clause to complete the thought ("Although it was raining" + ", I walked to school.").

02 · Word Forms · 词形

A word family is a group of words that share meaning and a root, appearing as different forms — noun, verb, adjective, adverb: perception → perceptive → perceptively.

ClassCommon suffixesExamples
Adjective-ful, -less, -ous, -ive, -tic, -able/-ible, -ybeautiful, endless, dangerous, creative, romantic, comfortable, noisy

💡 Choose the form that matches the word's job in the sentence: subject, action, description, or manner. · 根据词在句中的作用（主语、动作、描述或方式）选择正确的词形。

Sample family: action (n.) → active (adj.) → actively (adv.)  |  decision (n.) → decisive (adj.) → decisively (adv.)  |  success (n.) → successful (adj.) → successfully (adv.)

03 · Comma Rules · 逗号规则

There are ten places a comma belongs, and five common misuses.

Ten Uses · 十种用法

  1. Before a coordinating conjunction joining two independent clauses: "It was cold, and we stayed inside."
  2. Between items in a series: "We are going water skiing, swimming, and snorkeling."
  3. After an introductory phrase: "To raise money for the cause, Mary sold her belongings."
  4. Around a nonessential clause/appositive: "Justin, who lives down the street, is going too."
  5. Between coordinate adjectives (no "and" between them): "the interesting, dangerous act of beekeeping"
  6. After an introductory dependent clause (if, when, although, because...): "If you want good grades, you need to do your homework."
  7. Before a quotation introduced by a speaking verb: Kevin said, "I like turtles!"
  8. After a transition word (first, next, therefore...): "First, we should eat tacos for lunch."
  9. Separating parts of dates and addresses: "On January 2nd, 1996, Bill Clinton, President of the U.S.A., visited Chicago, Illinois."
  10. Before a name in direct address: "Would you please be quiet, Tony?"

Five Misuses · 五种误用

❌ Don't put a comma: between subject and verb · between only two items joined by "and" · around an essential (restrictive) clause · before "and"/"but" joining only two words · between two independent clauses with no conjunction (comma splice). · 不要在主谓之间、仅两项由"and"连接时、限定性从句两侧、仅连接两个词的"and/but"前，或无连词的两个独立分句之间加逗号。

04 · Conjunctions · 连词

Coordinating — FANBOYS

Connect equal ideas: forandnorbutoryetso. Put a comma before FANBOYS when it joins two independent clauses; no comma when it joins only two words or phrases.

Correlative — work in pairs

both...andeither...orneither...nornot only...but alsowhether...or

Subordinating — join a dependent clause to a main clause

althoughbecausesincewhenwhileunlessuntilas soon as

💡 If the second idea depends on the first, choose a subordinator; if it contrasts, choose yet or although; if it offers a choice, choose or or either...or.

05 · Quotation Marks · 引号

  1. Introduce a quote with a dialogue tag + comma: Danny said, "I'm leaving soon."
  2. Introduce with a complete sentence + colon: Kennedy said: "Ask not what your country can do for you..."
  3. Blend a quote into your own sentence — no comma, no colon.
  4. A short quoted phrase inside your sentence takes quotation marks only, no comma.
  5. A dialogue tag interrupting a quote — the second part starts lowercase: "I don't know," he said, "but the law is the law."
  6. Periods, commas, question marks and exclamation points go inside the closing quotation mark.
📌 "What a great day!" yelled Mr. Dobbins. — note the exclamation point sits inside the closing mark. · 感叹号在右引号内。
