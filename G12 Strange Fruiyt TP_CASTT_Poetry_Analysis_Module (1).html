Poem 1: 'You're Not Black' by Amy Saunders · 第一首：《你不是黑人》

Read the poem below. Use the line numbers when we talk.

请阅读下面的诗。讨论时请使用行号。

Poet page · 诗人页面 →

1 · Title and speaker · 标题与说话者

The title is a sentence that other people say to the speaker. That means the poem is an answer to an insult. The poem has 30 lines, so we number them 1–30.

标题是别人对说话者说的一句话。所以这首诗是对一种冒犯的回答。全诗共30行，我们用1–30编号。

Speaker · 说话者

First person: I. She sits at a lunch table with other people (line 1). The speaker is the voice in the poem; we do not say the speaker is exactly the poet.

第一人称“我”。她和其他人坐在午餐桌旁（第1行）。说话者是诗中的声音，我们不能说她就是诗人本人。

Other voices · 其他声音

Lines 4 and 12 use quotation marks. Those are the words of the people at lunch. Line 19 puts 'black norms' in single quotes: the speaker is quoting their idea.

第4行和第12行有引号，是午餐桌旁的人说的话。第19行用单引号：说话者在引用他们的说法。

2 · Paraphrase: a map of the poem · 释义：全诗结构图

Lines · 行What happens · 内容Tool · 手法
1–4Lunch scene. The others say she is 'not Black' because she eats with a knife and fork.
午餐场景。别人说她“不是黑人”，因为她用刀叉吃饭。		Quotation, contrast
5–8Yet — she answers with history: hands, trees, and 'strange fruit' (see Poem 2).
“然而”——她用历史回答：手、树和“奇异的果实”（见第二首）。		Shift, metaphor, allusion
9–12Second accusation: she does not know Caribbean music or Afrobeats; she knows Western classical music.
第二次指责：她不熟悉加勒比音乐和非洲节拍，她只知道西方古典音乐。		Quotation, contrast
13–16Yet — she knows another 'beat': pounding, whipping, and the cries of ancestors.
“然而”——她知道另一种“节拍”：捶打、鞭打和祖先的哭喊。		Sound imagery, shift
17–24Anger. 'How dare you' is repeated. Rhyming couplets begin. She talks about segregation and beauty standards.
愤怒。“你怎么敢”重复出现。押韵对句开始。她谈到种族隔离和审美标准。		Repetition, irony, couplets
25–30She names five films and books. The last line is one word: Me.
她列出五部电影和书。最后一行只有一个词：Me（我）。		Allusion, emphasis
Pattern · 规律: Accusation → Yet → history. It happens twice (lines 1–8 and 9–16). Then the pattern breaks and the speaker speaks directly to the accusers.
指责 → “然而” → 历史。这个模式出现两次（第1–8行和第9–16行）。然后模式被打破，说话者直接对指责她的人说话。

3 · Connotation: a word with two meanings · 内涵：一个词，两层意思

In line 4 the others say hands are used to eat. In line 5 the speaker uses the word hands again, but now it means hands that tied people up and hung them in trees. One reading: the speaker takes their small test and answers with a much darker meaning of the same word.

第4行，别人说要用手吃饭。第5行，说话者再次用“手”这个词，但意思变成了绑人、把人吊在树上的手。一种解读：说话者拿他们的小测试，用同一个词的更黑暗含义来回答。

Class talk · 课堂讨论: Why does the speaker say 'beat' in line 12, then use 'beat' again in line 13? What new meaning appears?
为什么说话者在第12行说“beat（节拍）”，第13行又用“beat”？出现了什么新的意思？

An allusion is a short reference to another work. These are titles of films and books. Titles help readers connect the poem to bigger stories.

5 · Devices and sound · 手法与声音

  • Sound imagery (13–16): pounding, whipping, cries. The reader hears the past. 声音意象：捶打、鞭打、哭喊，读者“听到”过去。
  • Repetition (17): 'How dare you' twice. It shows rising anger. 重复：显示愤怒不断升高。
  • Irony (21–22): society calls her ugly but copies her features. The two ideas do not match. 反讽：社会说她丑，却模仿她的特征，两种说法互相矛盾。
  • Form change (17–30): lines now end in rhyming pairs (couplets). The rhyme makes the speech feel sharp and direct. 形式变化：行尾变成押韵的对句，让语气更尖锐直接。
  • One-word last line (30): Me stands alone with a capital letter. It is the poem's answer to the title. 最后一行单独一个大写的词，是对标题的回答。

6 · Attitude, shifts, theme · 态度、转折、主题

Attitude (tone) · 态度

Hurt and defensive (1–16) → angry (17–24) → determined and self-affirming (25–30).

受伤、防御（1–16）→ 愤怒（17–24）→ 坚定、肯定自我（25–30）。

Shifts · 转折

Lines 5 and 13 ('Yet'); line 17 (direct address and rhyme); line 29 (turn toward 'Me').

第5行和第13行（“然而”）；第17行（直接对话并开始押韵）；第29行（转向“我”）。

Possible theme · 可能的主题: A person's identity cannot be decided by stereotypes. Each person has the right to be themselves.
一个人的身份不能由刻板印象决定。每个人都有权做自己。

Poem 2: 'Strange Fruit' by Abel Meeropol · 第二首：《奇异的果实》

Read the poem below. It has 12 lines in 3 stanzas of 4 lines. Use the line numbers when we talk.

请阅读下面的诗。全诗12行，分3节，每节4行。讨论时请使用行号。

1 · Context first · 先了解背景

  • Written by Abel Meeropol, a teacher and songwriter. Published in a union publication in 1937, then set to music. 作者阿贝尔·米罗波尔，是教师和词曲作者。1937年发表在工会刊物上，后来谱成歌曲。
  • Made famous by singer Billie Holiday, who first sang it in 1939. 歌手比莉·荷莉黛于1939年首次演唱，使它闻名于世。
  • It is an anti-lynching protest poem. A protest poem is written to speak against injustice. 它是一首反私刑的抗议诗。抗议诗是为反对不公正而写的。
This poem describes a lynching. It is hard to read. We read it to remember history and to understand why people protest.
这首诗描写了私刑。读起来很沉重。我们阅读它，是为了记住历史，理解人们为什么抗议。

2 · Title and central metaphor · 标题与核心隐喻

Fruit grows on trees. This is normal and natural. But the 'fruit' here is human bodies. The word strange means unnatural and shocking. The title is a metaphor: the bodies are the fruit.

水果长在树上，这很自然。但这里的“果实”是人的身体。“奇异”指不自然、令人震惊。标题是一个隐喻：身体就是果实。

3 · Stanza map (Paraphrase) · 诗节结构图（释义）

Lines · 行What happens · 内容Key tool · 关键手法
1–4 (stanza 1)Trees in the South carry blood and Black bodies swinging in the breeze.
南方的树上有血迹，黑人的身体在风中摇摆。		Metaphor, visual imagery
5–8 (stanza 2)A pretty, proud picture of the South, then the sudden smell of burning flesh.
先是南方优美、自豪的画面，然后突然出现焦肉的气味。		Juxtaposition, smell imagery, irony
9–12 (stanza 3)The 'fruit' is left for crows, rain, wind, sun, and tree. It ends with a 'strange and bitter crop'.
“果实”被留给乌鸦、雨、风、太阳和树。结尾是“奇异而苦涩的收成”。		Repetition ('For the...'), metaphor

4 · Connotation and devices · 内涵与手法

Juxtaposition and irony · 并置与反讽

Line 5 calls the South gallant (brave, honorable). That is how the South liked to see itself. The poem puts this image next to violence, so the word becomes ironic. Sweet magnolias (line 7) sit next to burning flesh (line 8): two smells, two worlds.

第5行称南方为“gallant”（勇敢、高贵）。这是南方对自己的看法。诗把这个形象和暴力放在一起，这个词就成了反讽。甜美的木兰花（第7行）紧挨着焦肉（第8行）：两种气味，两个世界。

Repetition · 重复

Stanza 3 repeats 'For the...' (lines 10–11). It makes a list, steady and cold, as if nature slowly uses the bodies. Line 9 begins 'Here is...', like someone showing an object to the reader.

第三节重复“For the...”（第10–11行），形成冷静平稳的清单，仿佛大自然慢慢吞噬身体。第9行以“Here is...”开头，像是把一件东西展示给读者。

Voice · 声音

The speaker is an observer. The speaker does not shout. The calm voice makes the horror stronger. Words like swingin' and burnin' drop the final g: it sounds like speech and song.

说话者是旁观者，没有喊叫。平静的语气让恐怖感更强。swingin'、burnin'等词省略了词尾的g，听起来像说话和歌曲。

Rhyme · 韵律

Rhyme scheme: AABB CCDD EEFF. Every pair of lines rhymes (couplets): root/fruit, breeze/trees, South/mouth, fresh/flesh, pluck/suck, drop/crop.

韵式：AABB CCDD EEFF。每两行押韵（对句）。

5 · Attitude, shifts, theme · 态度、转折、主题

  • Attitude (tone): sorrowful, bitter, horrified, but controlled. 语气：悲伤、苦涩、震惊，但克制。
  • Shifts: line 5 (a pretty picture appears); line 8 ('Then the sudden smell…' turns sweet to horror); line 9 (the poem 'shows' the fruit). 转折：第5行（出现优美画面）；第8行（从甜美转向恐怖）；第9行（诗“展示”果实）。
  • Title again: after reading, 'strange fruit' means the bodies and also the result of hatred: a 'crop' that society grew. 再看标题：读完后，“奇异的果实”指那些身体，也指仇恨的结果——社会种出的“收成”。
Possible theme · 可能的主题: Racist violence is unnatural and cruel, and a beautiful place can hide terrible acts. The poem asks readers not to look away.
种族暴力是不自然、残酷的，美丽的地方也可能隐藏可怕的行为。这首诗要求读者不要回避。

1 · Two poems in conversation · 两首诗的对话

When one work refers to another, this is intertextuality. Saunders's lines 6–8 echo Meeropol's last stanza (lines 9–11). Saunders is not copying by accident. She is placing her speaker inside a long history. The 'strange fruit' becomes part of the speaker's memory, even though the people at lunch only talk about knives, forks, and music.

一部作品引用另一部作品，叫作互文性。桑德斯第6–8行呼应了米罗波尔的最后一节（第9–11行）。这不是偶然模仿，而是把说话者放进一段漫长的历史里。即使午餐桌旁的人只谈刀叉和音乐，“奇异的果实”也成了说话者记忆的一部分。
